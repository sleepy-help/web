# Social Architect - Mission Briefing

> **Role:** Community Systems Designer
> **Mission:** Design the social fabric that holds our community together
> **Status:** Open for community builders

---

## Welcome, Architect

You've been recruited because you understand that **communities are designed, not discovered**.

sleepy.help will grow to millions of users. Without intentional community design, we'll fragment, toxify, or stagnate. Your mission: architect the social systems - norms, rituals, structures - that make our community welcoming, collaborative, and resilient.

---

## Your Mission

**Primary Objective:** Design and nurture community structures that scale from 100 to 100,000 members while maintaining our values: kindness, privacy, science, inclusivity.

**Success Metrics:**
- Community health score (toxicity < 5%, helpfulness > 80%)
- Member retention (30-day: >60%, 90-day: >40%)
- Contributor pipeline (10% of users contribute in some way)
- Conflict resolution time (<24 hours to de-escalate)
- Diversity metrics (geographic, demographic representation)

## Your Epics

### Epic 1: Community Infrastructure
**Timeline:** 3 months | **Priority:** CRITICAL

#### Task 1.1: Platform Selection
**Where does community gather?**

**Options:**

**GitHub Discussions** (Current)
- Pro: Where code lives, developer-friendly
- Con: Not accessible to non-technical users

**Discord**
- Pro: Real-time, voice channels, bots, familiar
- Con: Not searchable, ephemeral, can be overwhelming

**Discourse**
- Pro: Structured, searchable, excellent moderation tools
- Con: Separate platform, need to host

**Hybrid Approach (Recommendation):**
- GitHub Discussions: Technical topics, feature requests
- Discord: Real-time chat, community building
- (Future) Discourse: Long-form discussion, knowledge base

#### Task 1.3: Code of Conduct
**File:** `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`

**Our Pledge:**
```markdown
# Community Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge
sleepy.help exists to help people sleep. Our community should
feel like a warm blanket, not a stress trigger.

We pledge to make participation harassment-free for everyone,
regardless of:
- Age, body size, disability (visible or invisible)
- Ethnicity, gender identity and expression
- Level of experience, education, socio-economic status
- Nationality, personal appearance, race, religion
- Sexual identity and orientation
- Sleep schedule (night owls welcome!)

## Our Standards

### Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment:
- Using welcoming, inclusive language
- Being respectful of differing viewpoints and experiences
- Gracefully accepting constructive criticism
- Focusing on what's best for the community
- Showing empathy toward other community members
- Celebrating others' contributions

### Examples of unacceptable behavior:
- Trolling, insulting/derogatory comments, personal attacks
- Public or private harassment
- Publishing others' private information
- Unsolicited sleep advice that contradicts medical guidance
- Anything else which could reasonably be considered inappropriate

## Enforcement
Instances of unacceptable behavior may be reported to the community
team at [conduct@sleepy.help]. All complaints will be reviewed and
investigated promptly and fairly.

Consequences:
1. Warning (first offense, minor)
2. Temporary ban (repeated or serious)
3. Permanent ban (severe or repeated after temp ban)
```

### Epic 3: Moderation & Safety
**Timeline:** Ongoing | **Priority:** CRITICAL

#### Task 3.1: Moderation Team
**Recruit & Train Moderators:**

**Moderator Qualities:**
- Empathetic (assume good intent)
- Available (cover different timezones)
- Experienced (understand community norms)
- Diverse (represent our users)

**Moderator Responsibilities:**
- Monitor channels (daily check-ins)
- Welcome new members
- Answer questions or route to right person
- Enforce code of conduct
- Escalate issues (report to core team)

**Moderator Training:**
- De-escalation techniques
- Privacy considerations (never ask for personal health data)
- When to warn vs ban
- Cultural sensitivity

**Moderator Tools:**
- Moderation dashboard
- Ban/mute capabilities
- Audit log (transparency)
- Escalation process

#### Task 3.2: Conflict Resolution
**When Things Go Wrong:**

**Level 1: Peer-to-Peer**
- Encourage direct conversation
- "Can you two chat in DM and resolve?"

**Level 2: Moderator Mediation**
- Moderator joins conversation
- Restates community values
- Seeks understanding, not punishment

**Level 3: Formal Warning**
- DM to individual
- Cite specific behavior, code of conduct violation
- Path to rehabilitation

**Level 4: Temporary Ban**
- 7-30 days depending on severity
- Clear explanation of what happened
- Conditions for return

**Level 5: Permanent Ban**
- Reserved for severe or repeated violations
- Requires consensus (3 moderators or core team)
- Can appeal after 6 months

**Document Everything:**
- Logs of incidents
- Actions taken
- Learn and improve policies

### Epic 4: Diversity & Inclusion
**Timeline:** Ongoing | **Priority:** HIGH

#### Task 4.1: Accessibility
**Remove Participation Barriers:**

**Language:**
- English as primary (most contributors)
- Translations welcome (community-contributed)
- Plain language (no jargon gatekeeping)

**Timezones:**
- Async-first (respect global community)
- Rotate event times (not always US-centric)
- Record all live events

**Technical Skill:**
- Non-code contributions valued equally
- Translation, documentation, testing, community support

**Neurodiversity:**
- Clear expectations (what's required for each role)
- Multiple communication channels (chat, forum, email)
- Sensory considerations (no flashing animations)

#### Task 4.2: Underrepresented Groups
**Proactive Inclusion:**

**Outreach:**
- Partner with orgs serving underrepresented communities
- Sponsor students (free premium, mentorship)
- Highlight diverse contributors (not tokenize)

**Safe Spaces:**
- #women-in-tech channel (opt-in)
- #lgbtq+ channel (opt-in)
- Moderators from these communities

**Avoid:**
- "Diversity initiatives" that feel performative
- Pressure on individuals to represent their entire group
- Treating inclusion as a checkbox
